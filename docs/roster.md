Selection rule (dragon #2): fame-ordered. A system enters the catalog only if it is (a) canonical in the complexity-science literature, or (b) instantly recognizable to an educated lay reader. Both is best. Nothing exotic before the giants are done.

## Pilot 10 (proposed — to be locked in Phase 2)

Chosen for maximal diversity across: physical ↔ social, natural ↔ engineered, spatial/temporal scale, and dynamical character (driven-dissipative, self-organized-critical, adaptive).

Per decision D14, the entries are the **classes**; the exemplars are designated evidence carriers and narrative anchors within each class entry, not entries themselves.

| # | Class (the entry) | Designated exemplar(s) | Why it stress-tests the schema |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Stars | The Sun | Self-regulating fusion; convection; the physical-scale extreme |
| 2 | Tropical cyclones | Hurricane Katrina (2005) | Canonical dissipative structure; short-lived; well-instrumented |
| 3 | Planetary climate | Earth (comparative: Venus, Mars, Titan) | Feedback loops, tipping points; class framing opens comparative planetology (D14) |
| 4 | Earthquake fault systems | San Andreas Fault | Self-organized criticality; Gutenberg–Richter statistics |
| 5 | River networks | Mississippi River basin | Fractal scaling; Horton's laws; slow structural self-organization |
| 6 | Cities | New York City; Tokyo | Superlinear scaling (West); the socio-physical bridge |
| 7 | Financial markets | New York Stock Exchange | Fat tails, crashes, adaptation; extreme data richness |
| 8 | Communication networks | The Internet | Scale-free topology; engineered-yet-emergent; singleton instance |
| 9 | Power grids | Texas Interconnection (ERCOT) | Cascading failure; engineered network at criticality |
| 10 | Languages | English | Zipf's law; cultural evolution; the least "physical" entry |

## Full roster candidates (~40 classes, for Phase 2 drafting)

**Astrophysical:** galaxies · star clusters · stars · protoplanetary/solar-system dynamics

**Geophysical:** planetary climate · weather systems / atmosphere · ocean circulation · plate tectonics & fault systems · volcanic systems · river networks · sand dune fields · ice sheets & glaciers

**Physical & chemical:** turbulent flows · convection cells (Rayleigh–Bénard) · chemical oscillators (Belousov–Zhabotinsky) · granular media & avalanches · plasmas · crystal/snowflake growth

**Socio-economic:** the global economy · financial markets · firms & corporations · cities · monetary systems (incl. cryptocurrencies) · supply chains · labor markets

**Infrastructure & technological:** power grids · the Internet · the World Wide Web · road traffic systems · air transportation network · global shipping network · software ecosystems (e.g., open source)

**Cultural & informational:** languages · writing systems · science (citation/collaboration networks) · legal systems · social networks (online) · Wikipedia · music traditions *(candidates pending scope review — cultural entries need the same rigor bar, not a lower one)*

Fame-first ordering within each category. The pilot 10 are drawn from across all six categories deliberately.
