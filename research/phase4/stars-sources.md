### instance_population
- **Milky Way star count: ~1–4×10¹¹ stars (commonly cited range 100–400 billion).** Source: widely cited range traceable to Gaia-era stellar-population modeling and galactic structure surveys; secondary consensus sources (NASA Blueshift; ESA/Gaia scientist J. de Bruijne interview; Wikipedia "Milky Way" citing peer-reviewed structure papers). **snippet-verified**, not primary-read (no single Gaia DR3 stellar-census paper fetched in full this session — candidate primary: Gaia Collaboration, "Gaia Universe Model Snapshot," A&A, arXiv:1202.0132, which was surfaced but not fetched in full). evidence_status: measured (order-of-magnitude consensus), level: class (Milky Way as the local galaxy).
- **Observable-universe star count: ~10²²–10²⁴ stars**, i.e., "order 10²³." Wide range reflects methodological uncertainty (galaxy luminosity function × assumed stars/galaxy). **Key primary anchor for the upward revision:** van Dokkum, P. & Conroy, C. (2010), "A substantial population of low-mass stars in luminous elliptical galaxies," *Nature* 468, 940–942, DOI: 10.1038/nature09578 — found elliptical galaxies harbor far more low-mass (red-dwarf) stars than assumed from Milky-Way-like IMF extrapolation, driving estimates from ~10²² up toward ~3×10²³ (roughly tripling prior estimates). **Snippet-verified** (abstract/summary read via search; full text not fetched this session). evidence_status: measured-untested (order-of-magnitude, method-dependent, explicitly flagged as uncertain even by the primary paper's own framing), level: class (observable universe, i.e., total stellar population across all galaxies).
- **Milky Way star-formation rate (instance formation rate, class-level): ~1–2 M☉/yr** (equivalently ~6–7 new stars/yr by number, given typical stellar IMF; some newer γ-ray/²⁶Al-based estimates run higher, ~4–8 M☉/yr converted gas-to-stars). Source: Kennicutt & Evans (2012), "Star Formation in the Milky Way and Nearby Galaxies," *Annual Review of Astronomy and Astrophysics* 50, 531–608 (standard review; accessed via NED summary page, https://ned.ipac.caltech.edu/level5/March15/Kennicutt/Kennicutt5.html) plus corroborating secondary press coverage of a 2021 γ-ray-based re-estimate. **snippet-verified**, review itself not primary-read in full this session. evidence_status: measured-untested (range spans ~4x depending on tracer/method), level: class.

### energy_rate_density (Φ_m)
- **Sun: Φ_m ≈ 2 erg s⁻¹ g⁻¹** — REUSED, primary-read provenance in research/phase1/quantitative-backbone.md §1: Chaisson, E.J. (2010/2011), "Energy Rate Density as a Complexity Metric and Evolutionary Driver," *Complexity* 16(3), 27–40, DOI: 10.1002/cplx.20323, Table 1 and confirmed in main text (L☉≈4×10³³ erg/s, M☉≈2×10³³ g → Φ_m≈2 erg/s/g). evidence_status: measured. level: exemplar.
- **Direct power/mass inputs (trivially citable, IAU nominal values):**
  - Luminosity: **L☉ = 3.828×10²⁶ W** (IAU 2015 nominal solar luminosity, Mamajek et al. 2015, arXiv:1510.06262). snippet-verified.
  - Mass: **M☉ = 1.98892×10³⁰ kg** (IAU 2015 nominal solar mass parameter GM☉ / G). snippet-verified.
  - Derivation check: Φ_m = L/M = 3.828×10²⁶ W / 1.989×10³⁰ kg = 1.925×10⁻⁴ W/kg = 1.925 erg s⁻¹ g⁻¹ (1 W/kg = 10⁴ erg s⁻¹ g⁻¹... note unit care: 1 W/kg = 10⁷ erg/s / 10³ g = 10⁴ erg s⁻¹ g⁻¹, so 1.925×10⁻⁴ W/kg × 10⁴ = 1.925 erg s⁻¹ g⁻¹) — **matches Chaisson's Φ_m≈2 to within rounding**, confirming the figure is self-consistently reproducible from IAU nominal L☉/M☉. evidence_status: derived (this cross-check is ours; Chaisson's own Φ_m=2 remains the citable published value).
- Chaisson also gives Φ_m for other stellar evolutionary stages of the Sun specifically (newborn ~1, subgiant ~4, red giant ~120, AGB ~2000 erg/s/g) and for a 10-M☉ star through fusion stages (H→O burning: 600→4000 erg/s/g) — see quantitative-backbone.md Table 2 for full reuse; these support class-general statements about "stars" beyond the Sun exemplar. evidence_status: measured, level: exemplar (Sun stages) / class (10 M☉ illustrative star).

## B. STRUCTURE

### fractal_dimension_spatial
- **Solar granulation (photospheric convection cells), perimeter-area fractal dimension: D = 1.25 for small granules (perimeter ~500–4,500 km), D = 2.15 for large granules (perimeter ~4,500–15,000 km); scale break at ~1″.37.** Primary citation: **Roudier, T. & Muller, R. (1986), "Structure of the solar granulation," *Solar Physics* 107, 11–26.** Relation: P ∝ A^(D/2) from measurements of 315 granules. **Snippet-verified** (abstract/results summarized via search of ADS/ScienceDirect listings; full-text PDF not fetched this session — recommend primary-read follow-up before final publication). Theoretical comparison value: D=4/3≈1.33 predicted for isotropic homogeneous turbulence in an isobaric atmosphere (matches small-granule regime reasonably well). measurand: photosphere granule boundary geometry (2D projected). evidence_status: measured-untested (single 1986 study; not independently CSN-style re-verified, though this is a geometric fractal-dimension claim, not a tail-exponent claim, so power-law-rigor rule applies loosely/by analogy only). level: exemplar.
- **Supergranulation fractal dimension** also has a published literature (e.g., "Supergranular Fractal Dimension and Solar Rotation," arXiv:2207.10490; "Solar supergranular fractal dimension dependence on the Solar cycle phase," arXiv:2202.00447) reporting cycle-phase-dependent D values for the larger (~30 Mm) convective scale — **flagged as located but NOT numerically extracted this session** (titles/abstracts only); follow-up needed for exact D values if the catalog wants both granulation and supergranulation fractal dimensions recorded separately.

## C. DYNAMICS

### extreme_event_statistics (solar flares — power-law tail WITH fit-rigor status)
- **Aschwanden solar-flare/nanoflare thermal-energy power-law slopes (3-D thermal energy model): α_E3 = 9/5 = 1.80 (theoretical), observed α_E3 = 1.80 ± 0.18.** Also reported: standard SOC model α_E1 = 13/9 ≈ 1.44 (observed 1.47±0.07); 2-D thermal model α_E2 = 7/3 ≈ 2.33 (observed 2.38±0.09). Primary citation: **Aschwanden, M.J. (2022), "Reconciling Power Law Slopes in Solar Flare and Nanoflare Size Distributions," arXiv:2203.12484, published companion in ApJL, DOI: 10.3847/2041-8213/ac7b8d.** Also foundational: Aschwanden et al. (2000) TRACE EUV nanoflare statistics (original α≈1.8 result referenced in the 2022 reconciliation paper). **Snippet-verified** (abstract fetched and read via WebFetch this session — this is closer to primary-read for the abstract/key-results level, but the full paper body/tables were not fetched).
- **Fit-rigor status per power-law-rigor rule: evidence_status = measured-untested.** The Aschwanden papers report theoretical power-law slope predictions compared against "observed" exponents with stated uncertainties, but **do NOT cite or apply CSN-2009-grade statistical machinery** (maximum-likelihood x_min estimation + Kolmogorov-Smirnov goodness-of-fit test against alternative heavy-tailed distributions). No CSN-style solar-flare dataset was found in Clauset, Shalizi & Newman (2009) itself (confirmed absent from the 24-dataset table in research/phase1/quantitative-backbone.md §2 — solar flares are not among CSN's tested datasets). **Conclusion: record α≈1.8 (or the range 1.44–2.38 across competing thermal models) as measured-untested, explicitly noting CSN-grade rigor has not been applied to solar/stellar flare energy distributions**, per filling_rules.power-law-rigor. This is a genuinely different situation from Gutenberg-Richter earthquakes (which CSN did test and reject in raw form) — flares have simply not been run through the CSN pipeline in the literature located this session.
- Broader context: flare frequency-distribution power-law indices reported across the field range widely, **1.35 ≤ α ≤ 2.90** depending on flare-energy proxy (thermal, non-thermal, GOES soft-X-ray, etc.) and instrument — this spread itself is evidence of measured-untested/method-sensitive status. level: exemplar (Sun) with explicit note that Aschwanden's reconciliation paper extends the same framework to stellar (non-solar) superflares across 13 orders of magnitude (cross-referenced in research/phase1/dynamics-criticality.md §2, "Self-Organized Criticality Across Thirteen Orders of Magnitude in the Solar-Stellar Connection," arXiv:2503.18136) — so this is potentially class-general (stars), not just Sun-specific, but the class-level claim needs its own fit-rigor check, not assumed inherited from the solar case.
- **SOC mechanism claim (separate per soc-two-claims rule):** the fractal-diffusive SOC (FD-SOC) model is PROPOSED as the mechanism generating these slopes and shows reasonable quantitative agreement, but per research/phase1/dynamics-criticality.md this is flagged with a live "dragon king" critique (extreme superflares may be a distinct population/mechanism) — record mechanism status as CONTESTED, not established, separately from the event-statistics claim above.

### temporal_correlation
- **Sunspot number / total solar irradiance (TSI): Hurst exponent H ≈ 0.7 (range 0.70–0.77 via detrended fluctuation analysis), indicating long-range persistence.** Primary citation: **Rypdal, K. & Rypdal, M. (2012), "Is there long-range memory in solar activity on time scales shorter than the sunspot period?," Journal of Geophysical Research: Space Physics 117, A04103,** https://doi.org/10.1029/2011JA017283 (also arXiv:1111.4787). **Snippet-verified** (abstract/results summarized via search; full text not fetched this session). measurand: sunspot number and TSI time series (activity-cycle-detrended). Caveat explicitly noted in the source: the ~11-year quasi-periodic component makes the series non-stationary, biasing naive Hurst estimation; DFA is used to correct for this, but the authors themselves flag large errors from short time records and multiple regime-dependent H values (evidence of non-universal/multi-scaling behavior, possibly reflecting multiple chaotic-attractor "centers"). evidence_status: measured-untested (single/small cluster of studies, explicit author-acknowledged estimation uncertainty). level: exemplar (Sun).

### chaos_sensitivity
- REUSED from research/phase1/dynamics-criticality.md §1 (primary-read provenance there, based on search-summarized sources): solar-cycle chaos is CONTESTED — some nonlinear time-series studies report low-dimensional chaotic signatures with short Lyapunov-implied horizons (one figure cited: ~15.9 months from sunspot-area analysis, explicitly flagged there as single-study/unverified), while others find no evidence of low-dimensional deterministic chaos, favoring a stochastically-forced nonlinear dynamo picture instead. **Do not report a single Lyapunov exponent as established fact for the Sun; record as rubric/hybrid with contested status**, consistent with the existing Phase 1 finding. Recommended anchor citation for the arbiter review: Petrovay, K., "Solar Cycle Prediction," Living Reviews in Solar Physics (2020, updated 2023), arXiv:2303.12648.

## My 3 biggest uncertainties

1. **Granule-count numerosity (~1–4 million) and the Roudier & Muller (1986) fractal-dimension figures rest on secondary/snippet sources, not primary-read full text.** Both are widely repeated in the solar-physics literature and internally consistent (D≈1.33 turbulence-theory match for small granules), but neither the original 1986 Solar Physics paper nor a modern granule-census paper was fetched and read in full this session — recommend a primary-read pass before these numbers are locked into the catalog with "measured" (rather than "measured-untested") status.

2. **The Aschwanden solar-flare power-law claim genuinely lacks CSN-2009-grade statistical treatment in the literature located this session** (no maximum-likelihood x_min + KS-test analysis found for solar/stellar flare energy distributions, unlike earthquakes or word frequencies which CSN tested directly). This is recorded as measured-untested per the panel-spec rule, but it's worth flagging to the orchestrator that this may reflect a genuine literature gap rather than a search miss — a targeted follow-up specifically hunting for "solar flare CSN power law Clauset" would be worthwhile before treating "untested" as final.

3. **Instance_population for "observable universe" star count (~10²²–10²⁴) is inherently soft** — it's not a single measurement but a derived estimate (galaxy luminosity function × assumed stars-per-galaxy), and the van Dokkum & Conroy (2010) IMF revision that drives the upper end of the range was read only at abstract/summary level, not primary full-text. The three-order-of-magnitude spread across secondary sources (10²²–10²⁴) itself signals this should carry evidence_status: measured-untested with the range stated explicitly rather than collapsed to a single number.
