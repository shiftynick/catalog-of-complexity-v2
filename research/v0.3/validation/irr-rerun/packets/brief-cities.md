## Scoping note

> The system is the functioning metropolis as an ongoing socio-physical
> institution: the built environment (buildings, streets, utility networks),
> the resident/worker population using and maintaining it, and the
> governing/economic institutions that plan, regulate, and finance it,
> exemplified by New York City. Inside the boundary: residents and workers
> acting as urban agents (out-of-scope(human) per D2), buildings and
> infrastructure, the local economy and land market, and municipal
> government as an internal adaptive institution (so zoning changes and
> post-crisis infrastructure hardening count as internal adaptation, mirroring
> the financial-markets treatment of exchange rule-making). Outside: the
> national/global economy generating demand and capital flows (forcing,
> recorded under relations.part_of), the natural hinterland and climate
> (forcing), and other cities in the urban system (recorded via
> instance_population and the Zipf-law class-statistical claim). Atomicity
> per filling_rules.atomicity-convention: individual residents/households and
> buildings/parcels are treated as the atomic parts at which the city's
> CHARACTERISTIC dynamics (scaling laws, land use, commuting) operate — not
> individual cells or neurons of residents, and not individual bricks of
> buildings. This is a dual-ontology choice, parallel to the english entry's
> speakers-plus-lexical-units split: residents are agents (out-of-scope(human)),
> buildings/parcels are the durable physical substrate. Scoring is
> class-general with exemplar evidence primarily from New York City; Tokyo
> evidence is comparative and much thinner (population figures only).
