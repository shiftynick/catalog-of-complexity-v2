## Scoping note

> The system is "a language" as a population-level communicative code: the
> shared grammar, phonology, and lexicon that a speech community jointly
> maintains and continuously reproduces, plus the utterances/texts that
> instantiate it. Inside the boundary: the linguistic structure itself
> (phonemes, morphemes, words, constructions, and the statistical
> regularities over them) and speakers' shared, distributed knowledge of
> that structure, insofar as it shapes production and comprehension. Outside:
> the individual speaker's full cognitive/neural apparatus (a biological
> system, out of catalog scope per D2), the cultures and institutions that
> use language as a medium (recorded only via part_of), and the physical
> transmission substrate (air, ink, screens, cables — recorded only under
> openness_dissipation). Atomicity per filling_rules.atomicity-convention:
> DUAL ontology, declared explicitly because a single atomic type would
> misrepresent this class (per the panel-spec's own worked note for
> "english"). (a) Speakers, as the interacting AGENTS whose usage decisions
> drive change — out-of-scope(human), not itself modeled; (b) linguistic
> units (phonemes/morphemes/words/constructions), as the REPLICATORS whose
> frequency distributions, co-occurrence structure, and diachronic change are
> what most of this entry's quantitative evidence actually measures. The two
> atomic types have distinct interaction sets (see interaction_ontology) and
> are not reducible to one another for this entry's purposes — a hurricane
> or a market can be scored on one atomic type; language genuinely cannot.
> Scoring is class-general with primary quantitative exemplar evidence from
> English; class-general numerosity/instance_population figures are recorded
> separately from English-specific ones and the level is stated on every
> value.
