**nonlinearity = 4 (rubric)** — **FLAG (fix).** The nonlinearity anchor's scale distinguishes 3 ("nonlinear interactions central... required to reproduce observed phenomenology") from 4 ("pervasive AND load-bearing at multiple scales/subsystems simultaneously... jointly responsible for formation, intensification, collapse, or pattern selection"). Every anchor actually scored 4 (tropical-cyclones, financial-markets) earns it through *several distinct, simultaneously-active nonlinear mechanisms* (WISHE + SST-drawdown + eyewall replacement; volatility clustering + herding + liquidity-feedback) — not one nonlinear kinetic mechanism manifesting in several guises. The BZ reaction's nonlinearity is essentially one thing — the Oregonator's autocatalytic (bromous-acid) kinetic term — whose consequences (temporal oscillation, wave propagation, Turing pattern selection) are multiple *phenomena* but not multiple independently-nonlinear *subsystems* interacting the way a hurricane's WISHE loop and eyewall-replacement cycle are two distinct nonlinear mechanisms. This reads structurally closer to the anchor's 3-tier (San Andreas, stars): "nonlinear core physics is essential... but not pervasive across multiple simultaneously load-bearing subsystems." The justification asserts the top score by analogy ("the closest available class analogue to financial-markets' and tropical-cyclones' own 4s") without actually satisfying the anchor's stated multi-subsystem test. Suggested fix: rescore to 3, or add justification specifically addressing why the single-mechanism-multiple-phenomena structure should count as "multiple scales/subsystems simultaneously" per the anchor text.

**feedback = 4 (rubric)** — Correctly includes the required structured loop list (per feedback.yaml's notes: "every anchor at score >=2 MUST include the structured loop list"). Applying two different resolution strategies to the same batch/CSTR ambiguity within one entry (blend-and-hedge for openness_dissipation vs. pick-one-exemplar for robustness_resilience) is an internal commensurability problem — a future cross-entry auditor comparing chemical-oscillators' Phi_m-adjacent openness_dissipation score against another regime-split system would not know which convention to expect. ## Summary of flags

1. **Fix** — `nonlinearity` scored 4 without clearly satisfying the anchor's "pervasive across multiple simultaneously load-bearing subsystems" test for level 4 (as distinct from level 3's "central to characteristic behavior, single mechanism"); the BZ reaction's nonlinearity is essentially one autocatalytic kinetic mechanism manifesting across several phenomena, structurally closer to the anchor's San Andreas/stars 3s. Recommend rescoring to 3 or strengthening the justification against the anchor's specific multi-subsystem language.
2. **Fix** — `openness_dissipation`'s class-general "blended middle ground" (2, with a hedge toward 3) resolves the batch/CSTR regime ambiguity by a different method than `robustness_resilience`'s exemplar-level split in the same entry; pick one convention for two-regime systems and apply it to both columns.
3. **Note** — `chaos_sensitivity` rests entirely on CSTR-regime evidence but is tagged `class-general` without the explicit regime caveat given to openness_dissipation/robustness_resilience; harmonize for consistency, not urgent.
4. **Note** — `modularity`'s justification cites "the tropical-cyclones anchor" for a modularity-1 comparator that does not exist in `modularity.yaml`; the actual (and factually correct) precedent is the tropical-cyclones class entry itself. Rephrase the citation, no scoring change needed.
5. **Note** — `robustness_resilience` = 0 is the catalog's first use of the anchor scale's floor value (no worked anchor currently sits at 0); the reasoning is sound and correctly distinguishes itself from the does_not_count clause, but flagging for awareness as a possible future anchor candidate.
