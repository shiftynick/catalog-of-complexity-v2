# Consistency Check — firms.yaml

Checked against: schema/panel-spec.yaml, schema/anchors/*.yaml (15 files), data/classes/financial-markets.yaml (closest structural sibling), data/classes/global-economy.yaml (reciprocal relation).

## Method

For every rubric/hybrid/categorical/structured score in firms.yaml: (1) locate the column's anchor file if one exists, (2) read the anchor's scale text, does_not_count exclusions, and boundary_clarifications, (3) check the firms.yaml score/justification against the scale text directly and against the nearest worked anchor (financial-markets, used throughout firms.yaml's own justifications as the explicit comparator), (4) check filling_rules compliance, (5) check level tags.

## Column-by-column findings

### decentralization (score 1)
Anchor scale 1: "strong central coordination actively steers system-wide behavior, though substantial dynamics remain autonomous... agents." Firms' justification (single controller with formal authority; large divisionalized firms delegate some authority) matches this band correctly, and is well-anchored to the anchor's own "infrastructure-category guidance" discriminator (real-time steering vs. bookkeeping-only) extended sensibly from ERCOT(1)/financial-markets(2)/the-internet(3) to a firm's CEO/owner as steering the whole organization in real time. No drift. **No flag.**

### nonlinearity (score 3), feedback (score 3, with loop list), decentralization, information_processing (score 3), adaptive_capacity (score 4), variation_selection, robustness_resilience (score 3), cascade_susceptibility (score 2), modularity (score 2), memory_hysteresis (score 2), tipping_transitions (score 1)
All of these are explicitly scored *relative to* financial-markets' own anchored scores, one point below or at parity, with the direction of each offset justified by a specific, checkable distinction (e.g., information_processing: firms process information for internal decisions rather than producing a Hayek-style public aggregate — a real, anchor-consistent distinction; robustness_resilience: correctly states the level (class/population vs. individual firm) per the column's own does_not_count rule requiring explicit level statement). feedback correctly carries the structured loop list required at score ≥2 (anchors' notes: "A justification that asserts 'score 3' without an itemized loop list is incomplete"). None of these contradict their anchor's scale text or does_not_count block. **No flag** on any of this group.

### openness_dissipation (score 2)
Anchor requires "every score of 3-4 should be traceable, even loosely, to a literal number in watts/joules/kg-s" — firms is only a 2, so this requirement doesn't strictly bind, but firms' energy_rate_density is unknown *and* openness_dissipation cites no literal figure at all (not even an "[unverified]" order-of-magnitude estimate), whereas its explicit comparator financial-markets cites a rough datacenter-wattage estimate even at score 2. The score itself is plausible (institutional throughput sustains operation without generating the phenomena of interest — matches scale-2 text), but the evidentiary basis is thinner than the comparator it's calibrated against. **Note** (not blocking): consider adding at least an order-of-magnitude placeholder or explicitly stating "no literal figure located, unlike the NYSE datacenter case" to make the comparison honest rather than implicit.

### chaos_sensitivity (score 1, evidence_status measured-disputed)
Matches financial-markets' anchor pattern (nonlinear dependence: yes; deterministic chaos: no) and correctly invokes filling_rules.no-market-chaos by extension/analogy, citing the sibling entries' own treatment. Reasonable extension since firms lack a dedicated chaos study of their own — the analogy is stated explicitly rather than silently assumed. **No flag.**

### criticality (score 1, mechanism_status: proposed) — FLAG
Two problems against the criticality anchor specifically:
1. **Boundary-clarification violation (events-not-structure).** The anchor's binding clarification states: "Static or structural scale-invariance NEVER counts toward this column... rank-size distributions belong to... extreme_event_statistics (measurand_type: static-cross-sectional). This column requires scale-invariant statistics of EVENTS generated by the system's own dynamics." Firms' own extreme_event_statistics column explicitly types the firm-size Zipf tail as measurand_type: static-cross-sectional. But the criticality justification states "Firm-size and growth-rate distributions show heavy tails (recorded under extreme_event_statistics)... **event statistics exist**, criticality-as-mechanism is weak" — this directly contradicts the anchor's own rule by treating a static cross-sectional rank-size distribution as if it counts as "event statistics" for this column. The growth-rate scaling (Stanley et al. 1996) is also not an event-size distribution over time; per firms' own temporal_correlation note it is "a cross-sectional-conditional-on-size relationship, not a serial autocorrelation/event claim."
2. **Unsourced literature claim.** "SOC-flavored proposals exist in the econophysics literature (Bak-Sneppen-style models of firm dynamics)" is asserted with no `sources:` entry and no `[unverified — model knowledge]` tag, unlike every comparable claim elsewhere in this same entry (e.g., March & Simon 1958 and Nelson & Winter 1982 under adaptive_capacity are both explicitly tagged `[unverified — model knowledge, canonical]`). Per the project's hard rule to mark model-knowledge claims as unverified, and per criticality.yaml's own boundary_clarification `published-claim-required` ("Level 1's 'informally observed or claimed' requires a PUBLISHED claim... A rater's own inference from adjacent literature... does not clear the bar"), this assertion needs either a citation or an explicit unverified tag.

Net effect: the final score (1) is very likely still correct once corrected — under boundary_clarification `mechanism-alone-caps-at-1`, a proposed-but-unsupported mechanism claim with **no** valid event statistics for the scoped system caps at 1 regardless, so the score does not need to change — but the justification text as written is inconsistent with the anchor it claims to follow and cites literature that isn't actually sourced. **Flag: fix.**

### emergence (score 3), self_organization (score 3)
Both scored one point differently from financial-markets (3 vs. 4, and 3 vs. 2 respectively) with specific, checkable reasoning tied to the anchors' own distinguishing language (order-parameter scope/quality for emergence per the `effective-theory-quality-gates-3` clarification; degree of current re-templating for self_organization, correctly keeping this column about genesis rather than control per the sibling-column note). Both plausible, non-contradictory placements given anchor text. **No flag**, though self_organization's claim that "no single 'exchange' analog re-templates the population-level pattern" is a judgment call worth a second read at a future audit pass — recorded as a **note**, not a defect.

## Level tags

All rubric/hybrid attributes are tagged level: class-general except extreme_event_statistics (level: exemplar(us-firm-population)), which is correctly scoped since Axtell's own paper explicitly declines to generalize the Zipf finding outside the U.S. ("The Zipf distribution may describe firm sizes in other countries as well, a conjecture..."). robustness_resilience is class-general rather than the column's stated default of exemplar — but the justification explicitly names and defends this choice (population-level reading vs. individual-firm mortality), which is exactly what the anchor's does_not_count clause requires when deviating from the default, and mirrors the identical choice already made in the verified financial-markets.yaml entry. No unjustified level drift found. **No flag.**
