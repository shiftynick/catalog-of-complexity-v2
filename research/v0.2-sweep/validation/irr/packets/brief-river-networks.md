# Blind IRR Rating Packet — river-networks

**id:** river-networks
**name:** River networks
**category:** geophysical

## Exemplars

- **id:** mississippi-basin
  **name:** Mississippi River basin
  **note:** primary worked exemplar; among the best-gauged large drainage basins in
  the world (dense USGS gauge network), and the system whose engineered
  near-avulsion (Old River Control Structure) makes tipping_transitions
  concretely citable for this class.

- **id:** nile-basin
  **name:** Nile River basin
  **note:** secondary/founding exemplar for temporal_correlation only — Hurst's own
  original long-range-dependence analysis (1951 et seq.) was performed on
  multi-decade Nile annual levels; no Mississippi-specific Hurst figure
  was located in this sweep.

## Scoping note

> The system is the dendritic channel network of a river basin as a
> self-organized transport structure: the tree of channel links from
> headwater tributaries to the mouth, together with the erosion-deposition
> processes that carve and maintain that geometry, over the basin's active
> geomorphic lifetime. Inside the boundary: channel links/segments and their
> confluence topology, the water and sediment flux moving through them, and
> the erosion/deposition feedback between flow and bed/bank geometry that
> sets channel form. Outside: precipitation and tectonic uplift (forcing),
> the floodplain/delta as a receiving landform (touched only where the
> literature gives a citable figure, e.g. delta lobe-switching period),
> human engineering works (levees, the Old River Control Structure — recorded
> as boundary interventions, not as internal adaptive components), and
> biological/vegetative effects on bank stability (out of scope for this
> non-biological catalog). Atomicity per filling_rules.atomicity-convention:
> individual channel links/segments between confluences — the level at which
> Horton's laws and Strahler ordering operate — not water parcels or
> sediment grains, and not individual meander bends. Scoring is
> class-general; exemplar evidence primarily from the Mississippi basin,
> with the Nile used only for the one column (temporal_correlation) where
> it is the field's own founding case and no Mississippi-specific figure
> exists.
