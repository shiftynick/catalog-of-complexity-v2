# Hostile Review — Complexity Science Lens

**Reviewer posture:** hostile referee, SFI / Ladyman-Wiesner-school. Target: the scientific content of the instrument (panel-spec.yaml v1.0-frozen), not its arithmetic. Read in full: schema/panel-spec.yaml, docs/decisions.md, docs/findings.md, research/phase1/SYNTHESIS.md, research/phase4/verification/cross-entry-audit.md, and four entries in depth (the-internet, languages, stars, financial-markets) plus tropical-cyclones (partial).

Severity key: **FATAL-if-unanswered** (undermines the catalog's core claim to measure complexity comparably), **SERIOUS** (a real hole a reviewer would insist on before publication), **QUIBBLE** (worth a line, not a redesign).

---

## 1. No computational/information-theoretic capability axis — FATAL-if-unanswered

**Claim.** The panel has an entire column family for *feedback, nonlinearity, criticality, memory* — the classic 1980s "complex systems" vocabulary — but nothing that asks whether the system can compute: no measure of representational capacity, no notion of a system doing something with information beyond "detect and respond" (`information_processing`, group D). Computation/information-processing capability (Mitchell's "what does the substrate DO with information," Crutchfield's epsilon-machines / statistical complexity, or even a crude proxy like channel capacity of the interaction network) is one of the two or three things a modern complexity scientist would insist separates "complex" from merely "nonlinear and high-dimensional." The catalog gestures at this in the `conceptual_layer` (statistical_complexity, excess_entropy) but explicitly refuses to score it ("mention-only… never as data," panel-spec.yaml:400-415).

**Exhibit.** `information_processing` (panel-spec.yaml:330-338) is a single 0-4 rubric ("detects, transmits, and responds… state-dependent way"). Compare `stars.yaml` (score 0, "nothing… evaluates a signal against a reference," lines 507-521) against `financial-markets.yaml` (score 4, "aggregates dispersed private information into a public statistic," lines 314-325). This is a real and useful distinction, but it collapses an entire research program (statistical complexity, predictive information, epsilon-machine causal-state counts) into one ordinal judgment call with no quantitative anchor and no attempt to operationalize even a toy proxy (e.g., mutual information between successive coarse-grained macrostates, which IS computable for time series the catalog already has — solar irradiance, price series, BGP tables).

**Existing defense.** SYNTHESIS.md §8 and panel-spec conceptual_layer explicitly justify the exclusion: no verified numeric value exists for any real macroscopic non-biological system for K(x)/logical depth/etc. This is honest but doesn't reach the weaker claim — some proxies (excess entropy on coarse time series, Lempel-Ziv complexity of symbolic sequences, even Shannon entropy rate, which the languages entry DOES compute for English, lines 594-604) are computable and the catalog simply didn't build a column for the tractable subset.

**Severity: FATAL-if-unanswered.** A hostile referee's first question to any "periodic table of complexity" is "where's the axis for computational capability" — Mitchell, Crutchfield, and Wolfram all treat it as load-bearing. The catalog's silence here isn't a gap in coverage, it's a gap in the *concept of complexity being operationalized* — the panel measures dynamical richness and adaptivity, not complexity in the algorithmic-information sense at all, and never says so plainly.

**Remediation.** Either (a) add a `predictive_information` or `statistical_complexity_proxy` column scored where a coarse-grained symbolic time series already exists in the catalog (languages has one; stars' sunspot series could support one), explicitly `evidence_status: derived`, or (b) — cheaper and more honest — add one sentence to panel-spec.yaml's meta block stating outright: "this panel measures dynamical/organizational complexity; it does NOT attempt to measure computational/algorithmic complexity, which remains conceptual-layer-only because no tractable proxy was judged worth the schema-freeze cost." Currently the catalog implies completeness it hasn't earned.

## 2. Double-counting: nonlinearity / feedback / chaos_sensitivity / criticality are four looks at one thing, scored as if independent — SERIOUS

**Claim.** Four separate rubric/hybrid columns all interrogate variations on "how strongly do small perturbations or couplings produce large, structured effects": `nonlinearity` (C, prevalence of nonlinear interactions), `feedback` (C, loop strength), `chaos_sensitivity` (C, sensitive dependence), `criticality` (C, proximity to critical points). The panel-spec itself flags the LLW caveat that nonlinearity is "neither necessary nor sufficient" (line 249) but doesn't resolve how it's supposed to be orthogonal to the other three in practice.

**Exhibit.** `financial-markets.yaml`: nonlinearity=4 ("multiplicative, threshold-driven interactions — GARCH-type variance dynamics, margin calls and stop-loss cascades," line 210), feedback=3 ("momentum/herding… volatility-liquidity spiral," lines 217-229) — the SAME margin-call/volatility-spiral mechanism is cited as evidence for both columns. `tropical-cyclones`: nonlinearity=4, feedback=4 — both citing WISHE (wind-induced surface heat exchange) coupling. There is no worked anchor text in `schema/anchors/` (checked nonlinearity.yaml, feedback.yaml, chaos_sensitivity.yaml, criticality.yaml) that gives a *procedural* rule for what counts toward one column and not another when the underlying mechanism (a nonlinear feedback loop) is literally the same phenomenon described from two angles. The four scores end up highly collinear by construction, then get fed into PCA as four independent dimensions (`analysis/run.mjs` SCORE_COLUMNS, lines 36-52), inflating their combined variance contribution.

**Existing defense.** None found in panel-spec or anchors beyond LLW's abstract caveat. The cross-entry audit (research/phase4/verification/cross-entry-audit.md) checked *ordering* violations within each column but never asked whether the four dynamics columns are actually measuring four different things across the roster — it is a consistency audit, not an orthogonality audit.

**Severity: SERIOUS.** This directly undermines PC1/PC2 interpretability (see attack #7): if nonlinearity, feedback, and chaos_sensitivity are 70%+ redundant, findings.md's "informational-adaptive vs. energetic-chaotic" axis is partly an artifact of having asked the same question three times with three different names.

**Remediation.** Either merge nonlinearity+feedback+chaos_sensitivity into a single "dynamical coupling regime" hybrid column with named sub-facets (a genuine simplification, though it breaks the freeze), or — cheaper — run a post-hoc correlation check across all four for the current n=10 (the pipeline already computes the full correlation matrix) and publish an explicit note in findings.md quantifying the redundancy, so readers don't mistake four axes for four independent measurements.

## 3. alphabet_closure ("flagship axis") is natural/engineered/social relabeled, and the project's OWN audit already proves it — SERIOUS

**Claim.** `interaction_ontology.alphabet_closure` (panel-spec.yaml:180-189) is explicitly nominated as "this catalog's nearest analogue to chemical valence" and a "candidate flagship axis." The vocabulary is closed/constrained/open. But per the project's own cross-entry audit (cross-entry-audit.md §3.4, lines 86-92): **"closed: 5/5 physical/geophysical/astrophysical entries; constrained: 3/3 engineered/institutional entries; open: 2/2 social/cultural entries… No entry breaks this gradient."** That is a perfect 10/10 correlation with `category` (panel-spec.yaml:80-84: astrophysical/geophysical/physical-chemical/socio-economic/infrastructure/cultural-informational — itself collapsible to physical/engineered/social). A column that never once disagrees with the pre-existing categorical label it's supposedly orthogonal to is not discovering structure; it is re-deriving the folk taxonomy the catalog already hard-codes as `category`. This is precisely the "chemical valence" analogy's failure mode: valence predicts behavior the periodic groupings don't already encode; alphabet_closure predicts nothing category doesn't already tell you.

**Exhibit.** the-internet is scored `constrained` with justification "same 'constrained' reasoning as financial-markets' exchange rulebook… sitting between a physics-closed alphabet… and an open one" (the-internet.yaml:232-240) — the justification pattern is "which bucket does this system's category intuitively belong to," not an independent measurement procedure. No entry's `alphabet_closure` value was ever computed from anything except the analyst's prior sense of whether the system is a natural/engineered/social kind.

**Existing defense.** findings.md treats the perfect correlation as a *finding* ("tracks the physical → engineered → social divide across all 10 entries with zero exceptions," line 33) rather than flagging it as a possible construct-validity failure. The one honest counter-evidence the project surfaces is adaptive_capacity breaking rank *within* the constrained tier (financial-markets=4 vs the-internet/power-grids=2) — but that's adaptive_capacity doing the interesting work, not alphabet_closure.

**Severity: SERIOUS.** If alphabet_closure is only ever category in disguise, it is not "this catalog's periodic-table valence axis" — it's a second copy of a column already in Group A, occupying schema real estate and PCA loading weight (findings.md PC1 loads alphabet_closure at 0.35, comparable to information_processing at 0.39) that properly belongs to category, a variable excluded from the numeric analysis entirely.

**Remediation.** Test the axis's independence directly: score alphabet_closure for a system that would embarrass the correlation — a designed/engineered system with a genuinely open alphabet (open-source software ecosystems, on the roster queue) or a "social" system with a closed alphabet (a rigidly rule-bound bureaucracy). If the correlation survives real stress-testing, promote the claim with that evidence; if the roster can't currently produce a disagreeing case, downgrade the finding in findings.md from "structural discovery" to "confirms the category variable was well-chosen," which is a much smaller claim.

## 4. The-Internet and Languages are not commensurable kinds with Stars — cross-scoring risks category error — SERIOUS

**Claim.** The panel asks every entry the same 30 questions and then z-scores and PCA's the results as though "spatial_extent," "hierarchy_depth," or "nonlinearity" mean the same kind of thing whether the referent is a plasma continuum, a protocol stack, or a population's shared grammar. For stars this is class-general dynamics of a single well-defined physical object obeying one set of equations. For the-internet, the entry itself declares **dual atomicity** (AS-nodes for structure, packets for traffic — the-internet.yaml:36-52) because no single atomic type describes the system. For languages, the entry declares **dual ontology** (speakers as agents, out-of-scope(human); lexical units as replicators — languages.yaml:31-45) for the same reason. Two of the four entries read in depth require the schema's atomicity convention to bend into a bifurcated structure just to be scored at all; stars needs a proxy atom that the entry itself flags as "the weakest link" (granulation cells standing in for a genuinely atom-less continuum, stars.yaml:29-38, cross-entry-audit.md:159). These are not three points on one axis of "how complex is this"; they are three different *types* of formal object (a field theory, a bi-layer network-plus-population system, a population-level semiotic convention) being forced through one 30-slot form.

**Exhibit.** `component_ontology` for languages literally has two unrelated countable populations (1.53e9 speakers; ~5e5 lexical units) with no principled way to combine them into the single `numerosity` scalar the schema wants (languages.yaml numerosity note, lines 149-153, retagged to exemplar-level specifically because no class-general figure could be produced). `hierarchy_depth` for the-internet counts AS-tiers (3 levels) while for languages it counts phoneme→word→phrase→discourse (3 levels) — both land at "3," a coincidence dressed as commensurability, since one counts a designed routing hierarchy and the other counts a linguistic constituency tree; nothing establishes these are the same kind of "level."

**Existing defense.** The atomicity-convention filling rule (panel-spec.yaml:62-68) requires each entry to *declare* its atomicity choice and says the consistency checker "audits comparable systems for comparable choices" — but auditing for internal consistency (is a hurricane's atom choice consistent with a market's?) is different from establishing that cross-scoring a field-continuum object against a bi-layer network against a population-semiotic system on the SAME numeric scale is meaningful in the first place. D14's classes-not-instances decision addresses a different problem (avoiding n=1 instance entries), not this one.

**Severity: SERIOUS.** This is the category-error risk named in the brief, and it is real: the schema's uniformity is a *methodological convenience* (one form, comparable columns) that the entries' own scoping notes keep confessing doesn't actually fit three of the four objects examined. The catalog doesn't hide this — the honesty is a genuine strength (see closing section) — but PCA/clustering on top of confessed-incommensurable atomicity is exactly the "you get out the axes you built in" risk named in attack #7.

**Remediation.** At minimum, findings.md should carry an explicit caveat next to every cross-system distance/clustering claim: "distances are computed only over the 15 rubric/hybrid + alphabet_closure columns, which are the columns most insulated from the atomicity/ontology-type problem; the 14 remaining quantitative/structured columns (numerosity, hierarchy_depth, component_ontology, etc.) are NOT commensurable across category boundaries and are correctly excluded from PCA already — but the rubric columns still implicitly assume a shared frame (e.g., 'decentralization' presupposes some notion of a controllable locus, which is a strain to apply to a field-continuum star)." This caveat mostly doesn't exist today.

## 5. Excluding biology (D2) removes the paradigm cases and the catalog knows it — SERIOUS, largely defended

**Claim.** Cutting cells, ecosystems, brains, immune systems (D2) removes the systems most complexity-science papers actually use as canonical examples (ant colonies, immune systems, the brain, evolution itself) — Holland's, Mitchell's, and most CAS textbooks' worked examples ARE biological. A catalog of "complex systems" that excludes them risks being read as "complex systems minus the ones everyone thinks of first."

**Exhibit.** The panel's own `variation_selection` column (panel-spec.yaml:353-358) is literally "variation-selection-retention dynamics" — evolutionary language borrowed wholesale from biology and then applied to markets and languages as the only "evolutionary" systems on offer, because the actual referent (biological evolution) is excluded by fiat.

**Existing defense.** D2 gives an explicit, defensible rationale (socio-technical systems are "the best-documented complex systems in existence; excluding them would gut the catalog" otherwise) and D3 frames the project as a reference catalog, not a claim to be THE definitive account of complexity science's canonical example set. This is a genuine, considered scope decision, not an oversight — it is stated up front, not discovered by the referee.

**Severity: SERIOUS but the mitigation is real.** The scope line is honest and the CLAUDE.md brief itself calls this "deliberate." A hostile referee would still dock the project for framing itself with the general word "Complexity" rather than "Non-Biological Complexity," since the title claims more territory than the D2 scope actually covers — a title/marketing problem more than a scientific one, but it is exactly the kind of thing that generates "wait, where's the immune system" pushback from the target audience (SFI-adjacent readers).

**Remediation.** Nothing scientifically required; cosmetic fix would be foregrounding the non-biological scope in the catalog's own name/tagline rather than only in D2, so the omission reads as a stated boundary rather than a discovered gap.

## 6. Missing: multiscale structure beyond a single hierarchy_depth integer; no universality-class concept anywhere — SERIOUS

**Claim.** `hierarchy_depth` (panel-spec.yaml:209-222) reduces "nestedness" to a single integer count of "organizational levels." Real multiscale complexity (renormalization-group thinking, scale-dependent effective descriptions, universality classes that group superficially unrelated systems by shared critical exponents) is absent as a concept anywhere in the panel or conceptual_layer. The catalog has `fractal_dimension_spatial` (a single spatial D) and `temporal_correlation` (a single Hurst exponent) but no notion that DIFFERENT scales of the SAME system might belong to different dynamical regimes, nor any attempt to ask whether two catalog entries share a universality class (the single most powerful unifying concept 20th-century statistical physics contributed to complexity science — the fact that magnets, fluids, and (contested) earthquakes can share critical exponents IS the closest thing physics has to a real periodic-table-style law).

**Exhibit.** The cross-entry audit itself independently discovered exactly this kind of multiscale problem and had to patch around it ad hoc: stars' hierarchy_depth needed a late fix to separate "convective aggregation" from "dynamo/magnetic organization" as orthogonal axes rather than one linear count (stars.yaml fix_log, lines 679-681) — precisely because a single integer can't represent two genuinely different multiscale structures coexisting in one system. river-networks' hierarchy_depth uses Strahler order and is flagged as incommensurable with the other nine entries' convention (cross-entry-audit.md §5.1) — the same symptom recurring.

**Existing defense.** None. Universality classes are not mentioned in panel-spec.yaml, decisions.md, findings.md, or the phase1 synthesis (search across research/phase1/* for "universality" and "renormalization" found no hits outside indirect discussion in qualitative-frameworks.md unrelated to this concept).

**Severity: SERIOUS.** A referee steeped in the SOC/criticality literature (which the catalog otherwise engages seriously — G-R statistics, CSN rigor, the SOC-mechanism-vs-statistics distinction) would notice the absence of "do earthquake magnitude statistics and any other catalog entry's event statistics share a universality class" as a glaring missed opportunity, especially since the catalog already collects tail exponents (`extreme_event_statistics`) across many entries — the raw material for a universality-class comparison already exists in the data and is never used that way.

**Remediation.** Cheapest fix: add a `findings.md` note comparing recorded tail exponents across entries (G-R b≈1, Zipf α≈1.95, financial-markets α≈3, solar flares α≈1.8) explicitly as a universality-class-style question — "do any of these coincide, and if so is it coincidence or shared mechanism" — this requires zero new schema, just an analysis pass on data already collected.

## 7. PCA findings are highly likely to be column-choice artifacts, and the project doesn't say so strongly enough — SERIOUS

**Claim.** findings.md's PC1 (informational-adaptive vs energetic-chaotic) and PC2 (violent dynamism vs distributed durability) are computed from exactly 16 columns: the 15 rubric/hybrid scores plus one encoded categorical (`analysis/run.mjs` SCORE_COLUMNS, confirmed by direct read of the pipeline). Given attack #2 (four collinear dynamics columns) and attack #3 (alphabet_closure = category in disguise), a large fraction of the 16-dimensional input space is redundant with itself or with the excluded `category` column before PCA even runs. With n=10 observations and ~16 correlated features, PC1 explaining "37.9% of variance" is close to what you'd expect from an essentially arbitrary rotation of a handful of underlying redundant axes — the finding that PC1 separates "informational-adaptive" from "energetic-chaotic" may be substantially a restatement of "some columns ask about information/adaptation and some ask about energy/dynamics, and those two clusters of questions are internally correlated by construction," not a discovery about the systems themselves.

**Exhibit.** findings.md itself is honest about n=10 being small and the inputs being "anchored rubric judgments, not measurements" (epistemic-status paragraph, lines 5-7) — genuinely good practice — but the caveat paragraph never mentions the *column redundancy* risk specifically, only the sample-size risk. "Leave-one-out sensitivity" is proposed as a check (line 7) but "leave-one-column-out" or "collapse the four dynamics columns and re-run" is not.

**Existing defense.** The epistemic-status paragraph is real and above-average honest for a v0.1 pilot. It just targets the wrong half of the "garbage in, garbage out" risk (rows, not columns).

**Severity: SERIOUS.** This is the "you get out the axes you built in" attack named directly in the brief, and on inspection it has teeth: the panel's own dynamics-group structure (four highly overlapping columns, as shown in #2) mechanically shapes what PCA can possibly find, and the flagship alphabet_closure axis loading onto PC1 (0.35) is suspicious precisely because it's redundant with category (attack #3) — meaning PC1 may partly just be rediscovering "physical systems differ from social systems," which nobody needed PCA to tell them.

**Remediation.** Re-run PCA (a) with the four dynamics columns collapsed to their first principal component as a single input, and (b) with alphabet_closure dropped, to see if PC1/PC2 structure survives. Report whichever result: if the structure survives, the finding is more credible; if it collapses, findings.md should say so before the roster scales to n=40 and the same artifact gets reported with false confidence from a larger n.

## 8. Classes-not-instances (D14) plausibly launders within-class variance into false commensurability — SERIOUS

**Claim.** D14 scores "tropical cyclones" as a class, not Katrina as an instance, on the stated rationale that class-level theory (potential-intensity theory) is what actually exists in the literature. But several columns are then filled with a SINGLE exemplar's numbers presented as if they characterize the class: `stars.yaml`'s entire dynamics section (nonlinearity, feedback, chaos_sensitivity, criticality, memory_hysteresis, robustness_resilience, etc.) is `level: exemplar(the-sun)` throughout Groups C/D/E/F, not class-general — meaning "stars" as a class is, in practice, scored almost entirely as "the Sun," a G-type main-sequence star, while the class nominally spans the enormous diversity of stellar types (giants, neutron-star progenitors, brown-dwarf-adjacent M-dwarfs) whose dynamics (convection zone depth, magnetic activity, flare statistics) differ by orders of magnitude. The class label promises class-general breadth; the actual filled values are one instance's numbers wearing a class-general credential in several places (e.g., `interaction_topology`, `decentralization`, `nonlinearity`, `feedback` ARE marked `level: class-general` in stars.yaml despite being justified entirely via Sun-specific reasoning about granulation and the solar dynamo).

**Exhibit.** stars.yaml, `decentralization` (line 326-336): `level: class-general`, but justification is entirely about "differential rotation, convective organization, magnetic cycle" — solar-specific dynamo language, generalized to "stars" by assertion, not evidence, since no non-solar star has resolved surface dynamics (the entry says as much in its own scoping note, line 36: "exemplar evidence is necessarily Sun-specific since the Sun is the only star with resolved surface dynamics"). The class-general LABEL is doing work the evidence doesn't support — this is an n=1 class dressed as a large-n class, which D14's own instance_population column (stars: ~1-4e11 standing population) would flag as *epistemically well-supported for class statistics* if actual class-wide data existed, but it mostly doesn't for the dynamics columns.

**Existing defense.** The scoping note is transparent about the Sun-only limitation. instance_population is correctly filled with the huge stellar population figure. But transparency about the limitation doesn't fix the mislabeling — `level: class-general` on columns whose justification is Sun-only is a mismatched tag, not just an honestly-flagged gap.

**Severity: SERIOUS.** This is exactly the "does scoring 'tropical cyclones' as a class launder within-class variance" attack named in the brief, and stars is the clean demonstration: a class with n~10^11 instances is scored, for most of its most interesting columns, as if it were an n=1 class, with the level-tag not reliably tracking that fact.

**Remediation.** Systematic re-audit of `level` tags across all entries specifically checking "does the justification prose actually support class-general, or is it exemplar reasoning wearing a class-general tag" — this is a mechanical, doable check (grep justification text for exemplar-specific nouns while level=class-general) that the existing verifiers didn't run (the consistency checker's own scope is score-ordering and enum drift, not level-tag/justification-scope matching).

## 9. Anthropic/fame-gated roster selection bias is acknowledged but its analytic consequences aren't — QUIBBLE-to-SERIOUS

**Claim.** Dragon #2 locks roster growth to "canonical in the complexity literature OR instantly recognizable to a lay reader" (decisions.md D12). This guarantees the roster oversamples systems that 20th-century complexity science ALREADY chose to study because they looked complex by some other framework's lights (SOC folklore systems: earthquakes, avalanches; CAS folklore systems: markets, the Internet). A catalog built this way cannot, even in principle, discover that "fame" and "complexity-by-this-panel's-own-metrics" are uncorrelated, because the sampling frame is fame. This is a real selection-bias problem for any claim stronger than "here is a well-documented reference for famous systems" — it specifically undermines any claim that PCA clusters or axis structure reveal something about complexity IN GENERAL rather than about the specific set of systems 20th-century popular science already agreed to call complex.

**Existing defense.** Dragon #2 states the rule as a deliberate anti-obscurity choice (avoid the "taxonomy rabbit hole" and "obscure over canonical" failure mode from a prior attempt), and D3 frames the goal as "definitive reference," not "unbiased sample for inference." This is a legitimate project-design choice, not a hidden flaw.

**Severity: QUIBBLE-to-SERIOUS depending on how findings.md's claims are read.** As a reference catalog, fame-gating is defensible and even sensible. As soon as findings.md frames PCA output as "the catalog's primary dimension" (definite article, universal-sounding) rather than "the primary dimension among 10 fame-selected systems," the selection bias becomes a live scientific problem, not just a scoping choice.

**Remediation.** One-line addition to findings.md's epistemic-status paragraph: "the roster is fame-selected (canonical-or-recognizable), not randomly or exhaustively sampled from the space of physical/social dynamical systems; axis structure describes this set, not a general theory of complexity." Currently implied by D12 but not restated where the PCA claims themselves are made, which is where a reader would need it.

## 10. decentralization vs self_organization: the panel worries about this pair explicitly, but the empirical near-collinearity (r=0.872) suggests the worry was justified and unresolved — QUIBBLE

**Claim.** panel-spec.yaml itself flags the risk ("Distinct from self_organization… anchors must enforce this," line 239). findings.md reports r=0.872 between them across the pilot 10 — the second-strongest correlation in the whole matrix. The anchors clearly TRY to keep the concepts apart (locus-of-control vs genesis-of-order) and produce one genuinely informative divergence (power-grids: decentralization=1, self_organization=1, i.e. NOT a divergence there either, actually — checking the matrix, the real divergence case cited is the-internet: decentralization=3, self_organization=2). But one clean divergence case out of ten data points, against a background r=0.872, is thin evidence that the two columns are doing meaningfully separate work at panel scale; it may just mean designed/controlled systems also tend to have less spontaneous order, which is a fairly unsurprising near-tautology (a system with a controller intervening has, by construction, less room for pure self-assembly) rather than a deep independent axis.

**Existing defense.** findings.md itself flags this pair as "the two columns the anchors fight hardest to keep conceptually distinct" and treats the correlation as expected-but-monitored (line 19), which is appropriately self-aware.

**Severity: QUIBBLE.** Already flagged by the project with roughly the right level of concern; the only gap is not yet testing whether the correlation is a near-tautology (controller presence mechanically suppresses self-organization opportunity) versus a genuine empirical finding about this roster.

## What this project does better than the existing literature

- **Provenance discipline.** Every value in every entry carries a citation, a read-status (primary-read / snippet-verified / unverified-model-knowledge), and an evidence_status distinguishing measured from qualitative from derived — far more disciplined sourcing than most popular "complexity science" surveys (Waldrop-style narrative accounts, or Wikipedia's complex-systems pages) ever attempt.
- **Refuses folklore on power laws.** The explicit CSN-2009-grade rigor rule, and the resulting honest downgrades (Internet AS-graph degree distribution recorded measured-disputed rather than repeating "scale-free" folklore; language Zipf fit correctly flagged as the ONE clean power law in CSN's own 24-dataset study) is better epistemic hygiene than most textbook treatments of these same systems.
- **Separates SOC-as-statistics from SOC-as-mechanism.** The `criticality` column's two-claim structure (event statistics vs. mechanism status) is a genuinely useful discipline that most popular accounts of "earthquakes are SOC" blur together; the catalog gets this distinction right where Bak-era popularizations didn't.
- **Self-auditing culture.** The cross-entry-commensurability-audit document this review leaned on is itself a rare artifact — a project that adversarially checks its own score matrix for ordering violations, reciprocity gaps, and measurand drift before a referee has to ask for one. Several of this review's findings (hierarchy_depth incommensurability, extreme_event_statistics measurand conflation) were independently caught by the project's own audit before this review started.
- **Names its own scope limits up front.** D2 (no biology), D14 (classes not instances), and the epistemic-status paragraph in findings.md are all instances of stating a limitation rather than hiding it — rarer in both popular and academic complexity-science writing than it should be.
