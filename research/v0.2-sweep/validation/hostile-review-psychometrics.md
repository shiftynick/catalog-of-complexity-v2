# Hostile referee review — measurement theory / psychometrics

**Reviewer stance:** demanding methods journal, adversarial. **Scope:** the panel-spec instrument (schema/panel-spec.yaml v1.0-frozen), its anchors (schema/anchors/*.yaml), the entries it produced (data/classes/*.yaml), and the analysis built on top (analysis/run.mjs, docs/findings.md). **Not in scope:** citation accuracy of individual facts (that is the citation-checker's job, already run per-entry).

Read in full: panel-spec.yaml; decentralization, self_organization, emergence, information_processing, feedback, nonlinearity, adaptive_capacity, criticality anchors (8 of 15); stars.yaml, the-internet.yaml, languages.yaml; docs/decisions.md; docs/findings.md; analysis/run.mjs; .claude/workflows/entry-pipeline.js (to establish who/what actually produces scores).

Severity key: **FATAL-if-unanswered** = the instrument's central quantitative claims (PCA loadings, correlations, clusters) do not survive peer review as currently supported. **SERIOUS** = a working paper would need a limitations paragraph and probably a methods fix before submission. **QUIBBLE** = real, worth a sentence, not disqualifying.

## Attack 1 — Ordinal scores fed to interval-scale statistics (z-scores, PCA, Pearson r on 0–4 rubrics)

**Precise claim:** `analysis/run.mjs` treats all 15 rubric/hybrid columns plus `alphabet_closure_encoded` as though the numbers 0–4 (and the closed=0/constrained=1/open=2 encoding) are on an interval or ratio scale. `populationStd()` (line 189), `pearson()` (line 195), and the PCA call (`new PCA(zmatrixForPCA, { center: false, scale: false })`, line 376) all consume raw or z-scored ordinals as if the distance from 1→2 equals the distance from 3→4 on, say, `emergence` or `decentralization`. `docs/findings.md` then reports Pearson r=0.952 for information_processing↔adaptive_capacity and PC1 loadings to three decimal places (0.39, 0.36, 0.35...) as if these were continuous measurements.

There is no justification anywhere in the anchor files or panel-spec for equal-interval spacing. The anchor scale text itself (e.g. `emergence.yaml` scale 0–4) describes qualitatively different *kinds* of macro-description at each level (0: bookkeeping aggregate; 2: morphological pattern language; 3: formalized order parameter; 4: multiple field-defining order parameters) — nothing establishes that the conceptual "distance" from 2→3 (acquiring a formalized order parameter) equals the distance from 0→1 (acquiring a routine statistical regularity). This is the textbook S.S. Stevens violation: computing means, standard deviations, Euclidean distances, and product-moment correlations on ordinal data.

**Severity: FATAL-if-unanswered.** This is not a footnote-level problem — it undermines the headline outputs (PC1/PC2 variance-explained percentages, the 0.952 and 0.872 correlations, the cluster dendrogram) that `docs/findings.md` presents as the project's "first cross-system findings."

**Existing defense:** `docs/findings.md`'s epistemic-status paragraph ("n = 10, and the inputs are anchored rubric judgments, not measurements... exploratory pattern description generating hypotheses, not established results") gestures at general fragility but never names the ordinal-scale problem specifically, and the precision reported (three decimal places on loadings, r to three sig figs) contradicts the caveat's spirit — a paragraph disclaiming rigor followed by rigor-flavored numbers.

**Remediation that would satisfy me:** (a) minimum — switch to rank-based statistics (Spearman rho instead of Pearson r, ordinal PCA / polychoric-correlation-based PCA, or nonmetric MDS on a Gower/ordinal-appropriate distance) as the headline method, with the current Euclidean/Pearson version demoted to a clearly labeled robustness check; (b) report both and show they agree (or don't) before trusting either; (c) at minimum, add one explicit sentence in findings.md naming the interval-scale assumption as a specific, named limitation, not folded into the generic "exploratory" disclaimer.

## Attack 2 — Anchor-calibration circularity: the instrument was tuned on the sample it now analyzes

**Precise claim:** Per `panel-spec.yaml` meta block: "FROZEN at Phase 5 (2026-07-02) after calibration against all 10 verified pilot entries." The anchor files are literally worked examples drawn from the same 10 entries the PCA/correlation analysis runs on — `decentralization.yaml`'s anchors are stars, power-grids, financial-markets, the-internet, languages (5 of the 10); `self_organization.yaml` uses tropical-cyclones, river-networks, the-internet, power-grids, languages; `emergence.yaml` uses stars, convection-cells (not in the 10), tropical-cyclones, financial-markets, the-internet, languages. `docs/decisions.md` D15 confirms: "Panel spec frozen at v1.0 after calibration against all 10 verified pilot entries."

This means the scoring instrument and the analyzed sample are not independent. Any structure PCA finds (e.g., "decentralization↔self_organization r=0.872") is partly an artifact of the anchors having been hand-tuned, entry-by-entry, until the scores "felt right" relative to each other — the anchor notes are explicit about this ("cross-entry audit, 2026-07-02" language appears repeatedly, e.g. in decentralization.yaml's "infrastructure-category guidance" note, added *after* seeing where power-grids/financial-markets/the-internet landed). The stars.yaml provenance log shows self_organization scored not against a stars-specific anchor (none exists) but "against the tropical-cyclones anchor... as the closest comparator" — i.e., cross-entry consistency was enforced by construction, then measured as a "finding."

**Severity: FATAL-if-unanswered** for any claim of discovered structure (e.g., the modularity↔self_organization r=−0.853 "surprise" in findings.md). It is not fatal for the catalog's use as a structured reference (D3's stated primary goal), only for its use as a hypothesis-generating empirical instrument — which is exactly how docs/findings.md frames it ("first cross-system findings," "generating hypotheses").

**Existing defense:** docs/findings.md explicitly flags "re-analysis after any anchor recalibration" as something that would "strengthen (or kill) these findings" — this shows awareness that anchors and findings are entangled, but does not name circularity as the mechanism, and does not distinguish "findings that are real signal despite circularity" (e.g., alphabet_closure tracking category, which is closer to a coding/definitional consistency check than a discovery) from "findings that are actually contaminated" (co-variation between two rubric columns whose anchors were cross-referenced against each other during calibration, e.g. decentralization and self_organization, which literally cite each other's worked cases in their own notes).

**Remediation:** Hold out entries from anchor calibration in the next roster expansion — score new (Phase 6+) entries using only the frozen v1.0 anchors, with zero further anchor edits, and see if the same correlation structure reappears in the held-out set. Until that happens, every correlation/PCA claim involving a column-pair whose anchors cross-reference each other (decentralization↔self_organization is the clearest case — the anchor files literally discuss each other) should carry an explicit "anchors were mutually calibrated; this correlation may be partly definitional" caveat, not just the generic n=10 caveat.

## Attack 3 — Single-rater-family provenance: every score, every verifier, and the anchors were produced by the same model family with no independent human check

**Precise claim:** `.claude/workflows/entry-pipeline.js` shows the entire production chain — sweep, draft, and all four "adversarial" verifiers (citation, plausibility, consistency, coverage) plus the cross-entry auditor — running as `model: 'sonnet'` (line: every `agent(...)` call in VERIFIERS and the per-class pipeline). The anchors themselves were "drafted by the orchestrator (Fable)" per the header comments in decentralization.yaml and self_organization.yaml. `docs/decisions.md` D10 states plainly: "rubric anchors must be calibrated carefully since the verifiers haven't been benchmarked against human judgment yet." That caveat is from the founding session and, per the file's own language, remains an open acknowledgment rather than a resolved item — I found no evidence in decisions.md, findings.md, or the research/ tree of any completed human-benchmarking pass. (`research/v0.2-sweep/validation/irr/packets/` exists as a directory but is empty — inter-rater-reliability work has been scaffolded, not executed.)

This is a monoculture problem in the strict psychometric sense: the "four adversarial verifiers" are not independent raters, they are the same model checking its own drafting model's work against anchors the same model family wrote, in the same overall session lineage, on the same day (2026-07-02 appears as the date on nearly every verification block). "Adversarial" here means differently-prompted, not differently-sourced. A halo effect (the model's prior on "the Sun is famous/simple/non-adaptive" or "financial markets are famous/complex/adaptive") can enter at drafting, survive all four verifiers because they share the prior, and then get reported as a robust, four-times-checked score.

**Severity: FATAL-if-unanswered** for any claim that verification independently validates a score (the D10 "quality gate: agents verify, human reviews flags" language implies a check that, on inspection, is same-family self-consistency checking, not independent verification in the psychometric sense of inter-rater reliability). **SERIOUS, not fatal**, for the catalog's narrower, honestly-stated purpose (D3: "the definitive... structured catalog," provenance-first) — a well-cited, self-consistent, single-source-annotated judgment is still useful as a starting point, provided it is labeled as such everywhere the scores are displayed, not just in one methods appendix.

**Existing defense:** D10's caveat (quoted above) is the one honest acknowledgment in the repo that this is unresolved. No defense exists against the halo-effect concern specifically — nothing in the anchors' `does_not_count` blocks addresses "does not count: the system's general reputation/fame for being complex."

**Remediation:** (a) Run a genuine IRR study: at minimum, 2–3 independent human raters (or, failing that, differently-sourced model families — e.g., a GPT- or Gemini-family rater with zero exposure to this project's anchor files) independently score a subsample of columns×entries against the *published, frozen* anchor text only (not against each other's drafts), then report Cohen's kappa or ICC per column. Columns that fail to reach acceptable ICC (e.g. <0.6) should be flagged in the app itself, not just in a research log. (b) Populate the already-scaffolded `research/v0.2-sweep/validation/irr/packets/` directory — it is the right place for this and currently empty. (c) Until (a) exists, every score display in the app should carry a "single-rater-family (LLM), unbenchmarked against human judgment" badge, not just a citation link.

## Attack 4 — Construct validity: decentralization vs. self_organization, and whether "emergence" is scoreable at all

**Precise claim (4a, decentralization/self_organization):** The two columns are explicitly designed as siblings measuring different constructs (locus-of-control-now vs. genesis-of-order), and the anchor files work hard to keep them apart ("Its sibling self_organization asks WHERE THE ORDER CAME FROM... A system can be centrally operated yet self-organized in its failures"). Yet `docs/findings.md` reports r=0.872 between them across the 10-entry sample — the single highest correlation in the entire panel apart from information_processing↔adaptive_capacity. Findings.md's own gloss ("the two columns the anchors fight hardest to keep conceptually distinct co-vary strongly") is an admission, not a resolution: if two constructs that the instrument's authors explicitly worked to separate collapse onto nearly the same latent factor across all 10 available data points, the most parsimonious reading is not "interesting emergent finding" but "these are not, in this instrument's operationalization, actually two different constructs" — i.e. a discriminant-validity failure. The catalog treats the near-collinearity as a discovery about the world; a psychometrician's first move would be to ask whether it is instead a discovery about the instrument (see also Attack 2 — these two anchors literally cite each other's worked examples, which is a second, compounding reason to expect collinearity independent of anything true about complex systems).

**Precise claim (4b, emergence):** `emergence.yaml`'s own scale is candid about its difficulty: level 2 vs level 3 turns on whether a "recognizable macro-level pattern" has graduated to a "formalized order parameter or macro-level effective law... used predictively/explanatorily by the relevant field" — a threshold that requires the rater to have and adjudicate domain-specific knowledge of what counts as "genuinely new effective variable" vs. "pattern-specific vocabulary" across astrophysics, finance, network engineering, and linguistics simultaneously. The stars.yaml entry scores 2 with a justification that concedes the mean-field dynamo theory's "independent quantitative content remains contested," while languages.yaml scores 3 largely on the strength of "a shared grammar... is a stable convention no individual speaker legislates" — a much softer standard of "formalization" than the convection-cells anchor's Ginzburg-Landau order-parameter case (score 3) or financial-markets' asset-pricing-theory case (score 4). The column's own notes admit an orchestrator-level rescoring occurred mid-calibration ("sun raised 1->2 and english raised 2->3 relative to the initial draft") specifically because the initial (arguably more defensible) draft scores didn't match the intended narrative arc of the scale. That is calibration-toward-a-story, a specific and well-known threat to construct validity.

**Severity: SERIOUS** for both — not fatal, because the anchors are unusually well-written and self-aware (the `does_not_count` blocks are genuinely good discriminant-validity engineering, see "done well" section below), but the empirical collinearity of decentralization/self_organization and the softness of "formalized order parameter" as a cross-domain criterion mean neither column should be treated, without qualification, as measuring a clean, unidimensional, cross-domain-comparable construct.

**Existing defense:** decentralization.yaml and self_organization.yaml both carry explicit "does_not_count" sections engineering the two apart conceptually, and findings.md flags the "off-diagonal cases" (ERCOT) as "the informative" part. That is a real defense against the charge of sloppy column design; it is not a defense against the charge that the *empirical result* the catalog is currently reporting (r=0.872 on n=10) is the wrong evidence to cite for "these are validly separable constructs."

**Remediation:** Report a discriminant-validity analysis explicitly: for the pairs the anchors work hardest to separate (decentralization/self_organization; emergence/criticality; adaptive_capacity/robustness_resilience), show the off-diagonal cases as the primary exhibit (as findings.md gestures at) rather than reporting the aggregate r as a finding about the world. For emergence specifically, either add a second, stricter operational anchor tier ("formalized order parameter with an independently-measured parameter and out-of-sample predictive test" vs. "field uses order-parameter vocabulary loosely") or explicitly downgrade cross-domain emergence comparisons (star vs. language vs. market) to ordinal-within-category only.

## Attack 5 — Commensurability across categories: is a rubric 3 the same 3 for a star and a market?

**Precise claim:** The catalog's central selling proposition (the "periodic table" framing) requires that a `feedback` score of 3 mean the same thing whether the system is a star, a market, or a language, because the entire point is cross-category comparison. The anchors attempt this via shared scale text, but the actual worked anchors are overwhelmingly drawn from a small, mutually-visible set of 10 systems, and several anchor notes show scores being set *relative to other anchors in the set* rather than against an independent absolute criterion: nonlinearity.yaml's languages anchor (score 1) justifies itself partly by "English is included in the catalog for other reasons... and nonlinearity is not where its complexity credentials live" — a comparative, narrative judgment about the entry's role in the catalog, not an independent measurement against the 0–4 scale text alone. feedback.yaml's financial-markets anchor (score 3, "not 4... outside crisis episodes... not obviously loop-dominated") and the-internet.yaml's own feedback score (2, in the entry body, justified explicitly as "one step below financial-markets' 3 and well below tropical-cyclones' 4") show scores being chained to each other's rank rather than each anchored independently to the 0–4 scale text. This is defensible as a practical calibration technique (relative ranking is often more reliable than absolute rating in psychometrics — that is a real, citable defense) but it also means the "same 3" claim is unverified: nothing in the repo demonstrates that a domain expert in solar physics and a domain expert in market microstructure, working independently from the scale text alone with no visibility into the other anchors, would produce the same number.

**Severity: SERIOUS.** This is the load-bearing assumption for the entire cross-category comparison enterprise (PCA/clustering across categories, e.g. the "the-internet's nearest neighbor is earthquake-fault-systems" finding), and it is currently supported only by within-project relative consistency, not by any cross-category absolute-anchoring evidence.

**Existing defense:** The panel-spec's `hierarchy_depth` column explicitly flags itself as "HIGH commensurability risk — the counting convention is what the consistency checker audits hardest," which shows the project is alert to this failure mode for at least one column; the decentralization anchor's "infrastructure-category guidance" note (real-time steering vs. bookkeeping-only as the discriminator, not "engineered systems land mid-range") is a genuine, well-reasoned attempt at a cross-category-portable criterion — better than most of the other columns get. But this alertness is not applied uniformly; most rubric columns have no comparable cross-category calibration note.

**Remediation:** For each rubric/hybrid column, add one worked "commensurability check" anchor pair explicitly drawn from *outside* category-adjacent comparisons already baked into the narrative (e.g., justify a star's feedback=2 against a language's feedback=2 side by side, independent of any third system), or run the same held-out/independent-rater protocol from Attack 3 stratified by category to see whether raters blind to the other 9 entries reproduce the current cross-category rank order.

## Attack 6 — n=10, up to 16 variables, PCA (p approaching or exceeding n)

**Precise claim:** `analysis/run.mjs` runs PCA on a 10×16 matrix (15 SCORE_COLUMNS + alphabet_closure_encoded). With n=10 observations and p=16 variables, the covariance/correlation matrix is rank-deficient (rank ≤ 9 after centering — though this run uses pre-z-scored, non-centered PCA, which does not evade the underlying degrees-of-freedom problem), loadings are unstable under small perturbations (leave-one-out would very plausibly flip sign/magnitude on several loadings), and the reported 37.9%/20.5% variance-explained figures for PC1/PC2 have no confidence interval and no stability check anywhere in the pipeline or findings.md.

**Severity: FATAL-if-unanswered** for treating PC1/PC2 as stable, reproducible axes (which is exactly how findings.md presents them: "PC1... the catalog's primary dimension separates..."). Not fatal for the narrower, honestly-hedged claim already partially made ("exploratory pattern description generating hypotheses").

**Existing defense:** findings.md explicitly names "leave-one-out sensitivity checks" as future work that "would strengthen (or kill) these findings," and states n=10 up front. This is the single best-defended attack in this review — the project has already identified the correct remediation, just not performed it.

**Remediation:** Run the leave-one-out sensitivity check findings.md itself proposes, before the next round of "findings" is written or before any of these PCA results are surfaced more prominently in the public app. Report loading stability (e.g., via bootstrap or jackknife) alongside the point-estimate loadings. Until then, PC1/PC2 percentages should not appear without an adjacent instability caveat, and no loading should be reported to three decimal places (false precision compounding the n=10 problem).

## What is done well (one line each, per field standards)

- **Explicit `does_not_count` sections on every anchor** are genuinely good discriminant-validity engineering — most home-grown rubrics skip this entirely and let raters improvise the boundary case.
- **Separating "event-statistics evidence" from "mechanism status" for criticality** (filling_rules.soc-two-claims) is exactly the right move to avoid the classic SOC overclaim, and is enforced consistently across entries (stars, earthquake-fault-systems, financial-markets all keep the two claims separate).
- **`evidence_status: measured-untested` as a first-class category**, specifically invented to hold "the CSN test wasn't run" (rather than silently rounding up to `measured` or omitting the caveat), is an unusually disciplined epistemic-status taxonomy for a project at this scale.
- **The `unknown` floor is used honestly and often** (e.g., stars/energy_rate_density's mass-figure rejection, the-internet's energy_rate_density, several columns in languages.yaml) rather than papered over — a project under any pressure to "complete the panel" typically starts guessing around this point, and this one visibly does not.
- **Findings.md's epistemic-status paragraph at the top**, even though (per Attack 1) it doesn't name every specific threat, is a real and unusually forthright gesture — most exploratory-analysis writeups bury the n=10 caveat in a footnote instead of leading with it.
